# Implementer's cold-read review of `plans/TITAN_NAV_PLAN.md` (revision 6, Opus reviewer)

Scope of verification: I read the full plan (1142 lines) and checked every named API, config file, and orchestrator path against the current source. Most references are accurate and correctly located — `center_resolution(event_key, axis='u')`, `search_window_for_obs`, `tukey_biweight_weights`, `add_model_error_floor`, `_spurious_result`, `bodies_in_extfov`, `stars_in_extfov`, `UCAC4_SATURATION_VMAG_LIMIT=8.0`, `NavReliabilityBreakdown` in `feature.py`, the geometry/flags/diagnostics unions, `DEFAULT_RELIABILITY_THRESHOLDS[TITAN_LIMB]=0.30` and its `config_540` twin, the `_titan_in_models` orchestrator branch, and `config_060_titan.yaml` having no code consumer all check out. The reliability-midpoint arithmetic (crosses 0.30 at D≈40.1 px / 42.3 px) is numerically correct. The math in Sections 2.2-2.4 (a_hat/c_hat orthogonality, min-incidence = sunward at all phases, no-double-count offset assembly, capsule-annulus symmetry) is sound. The findings below are the places that would actually cost me time or send me down a wrong path.

## 1. AMBIGUOUS

**A1. The occluder-helper signature cannot deliver the "bit-identical" guarantee it promises.**
Section 2.1 item 5 / Section 3 table: *"Phase B extracts it into a module-level helper in `nav_model_body.py` with signature `occluder_mask_for_body(obs, body_name, bbox_ext, *, config) -> NDArrayBoolType | None` ... Behavior of `NavModelBody` must be bit-identical after the refactor."*
The current `_compute_occluder_local` (`nav_model_body.py:749`) takes a **pre-built oversampled backplane** `restr_bp` (built at `nav_model_body.py:585`, shared with the incidence/lambert/resolution reads), plus `oversample_v/oversample_u`, and consumes `self._siblings` and `self._subject_range_km`; it does `where_in_front` on the oversampled grid then `filter_downsample(...) >= 0.5`. The proposed signature exposes none of these. Two readings:
- (a) Take the plan's signature literally → the helper must rebuild its own backplane and re-derive oversample factors and the sibling/range list internally. That diverges from the shared-`restr_bp` structure and, unless the oversample recomputation and downsample are reproduced exactly, is **not** bit-identical — directly contradicting the guarantee.
- (b) Thread `restr_bp` (or the meshgrid + oversample factors + siblings) through the helper so `NavModelBody` passes its existing objects and stays identical, while `NavModelTitan` builds its own over the mask bbox.
I would pick (b) and treat the plan's stated signature as an error — **medium-high confidence**. This needs an operator/plan correction because it changes a published signature.

**A2. `W` is defined as `max(search_window_for_obs(context))` but called the "pointing search half-window."**
Section 2.1 item 6. `search_window_for_obs` (`nav_technique.py:126`) returns `(margin_v, margin_u)` — the fixed per-instrument **extfov margin**, documented for at-edge detection, not a pointing-uncertainty search radius. Two readings: (a) `W` really is just the extfov margin (a fixed constant), or (b) `W` should scale with actual pointing uncertainty. The plan clearly intends (a) ("the same bound every other technique uses"), so I'd implement `W = max(extfov_margin_vu)` — **high confidence** — but the "search half-window" naming invites a reader to look for a different quantity that doesn't exist.

## 2. CONFUSING

**C1. The five-revision preamble (lines 13-40) front-loads normative consequences before the method exists.** A cold reader hits "along-track-only dilation in pass 1, residual pad after recenter," "star-locked frames become Phase E truth anchors," and the double-count rule before Section 2 has established what a pass, an annulus, or `c_sub` even is. This is normative content living in a changelog. It would land far better as a one-line "revised through rev 6; all normative behavior is in Section 2" with the substance left in Section 2 only.

**C2. The mask hypothesis-riding rule is scattered across five locations.** The exact per-pass semantics (which way the mask is shifted, which axis it's dilated on, by how much) are spread over Section 2.1 item 5, Section 2.2.1, Section 2.2.2, Section 2.3.2, and the `symmetry_scan`/`radial_profiles` signatures (`contaminant_mask` + `pass_pad_px` + `mask_shift_vu`). Reconstructing "pass 1: shift by `c`, dilate along `t` by `W`; recenter pass: shift by accumulated `(c,d)`, dilate by `recenter_threshold_px`" requires cross-referencing all five. Under time pressure I would likely get the recenter-pass dilation amount or the shift argument wrong. A single 3-row table (pass × {mask shift, dilation axis+amount, annulus shape}) would make this land the first time and is the single highest-value edit to the document.

**C3. Symbol density in the double-count region.** `c` / `c_sub` / `c'` / `c_true`, `d` / `d1` / `d2`, `q` (mirror displacement) vs `t`/`s` (grid axes) vs `theta`. All are defined, but Section 2.4's "final pass's `c_sub` REPLACES pass-1's while `d` accumulates" demands the reader actively re-derive why (recenter translates the grid along `a_hat` only). Correct, but demanding; a one-line worked example (`offset = c_sub2·c_hat + (d1+d2)·a_hat`) beside the prose would remove the risk.

**C4. "ConfidenceSpec class attribute mirroring BodyBlobNav" is imprecise about the mechanism.** `BodyBlobNav` does not define a literal `confidence_spec` (it defaults to `None` in the base at `nav_technique.py:271`); the spec is **loaded from `config_510_techniques.yaml`** via `load_confidence_spec` (`confidence_config.py:36`). The Section 3 table does mention putting coefficients in config_510, which resolves it — but the Section 2.4 phrasing "class attribute" would send me looking for a hardcoded ClassVar that isn't the pattern.

## 3. INCORRECT

**I1. The `occluder_mask_for_body` signature is internally inconsistent with the bit-identical requirement** — see A1. This is the one place I'm confident the plan is factually wrong as written (the signature omits the oversampled backplane, oversample factors, and sibling/range state that the extracted behavior depends on). Everything else I checked in the "INCORRECT" spirit held up.

**I2. The prescribed `stars_in_extfov` call for YBSC is missing a required argument.** Section 2.1 item 5: *"`catalog_name='ybsc'` for `mag_max=6.5`."* The real signature (`catalog.py:414`) is `stars_in_extfov(obs, config, *, catalog_name, mag_min, mag_max, radec_movement=None)` — `mag_min` is keyword-only with **no default**. The YBSC call as written raises `TypeError`. Minor (the fix is obvious — pass some `mag_min`, e.g. a very bright floor), but the plan gives no value and the Tycho-2 call is the only one it spells out completely.

## 4. MISSING

**M1. How to unit-test the model's decline gates without a real obs (Phase B).** This is the largest gap. Phase B says model decline-gate tests use *"the `FakeObs` fixture pattern from `conftest.py`; do not instantiate a real `ObsSnapshot`."* But `FakeObs` (`tests/spindoctor/nav_technique/conftest.py:70`) exposes **only** `extfov_margin_vu`. The model's `create_model` (Section 2.1) needs `obs.inventory`, `oops.Backplane(...).incidence_angle`, `center_resolution`, `where_in_front`, ring backplanes, `obs.ra_dec_limits_ext`, `obs.star_min/max_usable_vmag` — none of which `FakeObs` provides. The plan does not say whether to extend `FakeObs` with a fake inventory/backplane, monkeypatch the geometry, or factor the geometry so synthetic radii/center/occlusion can be injected. The gates (`too_small`, `not_fully_visible`, `occluded`) are defined purely in terms of oops-derived quantities, so there is no obvious no-oops path. An implementer will stall here.

**M2. Placeholder confidence-spec coefficients (Phase B).** The plan says anchors "start at Phase-A placeholders" but supplies no numbers and no config-block shape for the five terms (`symmetry_peak_score`, `symmetry_valid_fraction`, `arc_inlier_fraction`, `arc_residual_rms_px`, `envelope_diameter_px`). `validate_registered_confidence_specs()` must pass at Phase B, so a valid block is required immediately. The shape is derivable from an existing config_510 entry, but no starting values are given.

**M3. Phase-D rendering of the symmetry-breaking axes is scope-hidden.** The plan lists new `atmosphere`-block truth keys (`interior_ramp_amplitude`, `ns_asymmetry_amplitude`, `ns_falloff_ratio` [explicitly non-affine], `axis_tilt_deg`, `sector_sharpness_gradient`, `cloud_blobs`) and says "extend the existing block" and "follow existing key-inventory conventions." But `atmosphere.py` currently renders a specific HG/`tau`/scale-height model (`atmosphere_spec_from_params`, `apply_atmosphere`); making, e.g., `ns_falloff_ratio` produce a genuinely non-affine per-hemisphere limb falloff, or `sector_sharpness_gradient` produce a `phi`-dependent limb sharpness, is substantial new rendering math that the plan only names. The acceptance gate (P95 bounds) depends on these actually rendering the effects they claim to probe. This is the second-riskiest under-specification.

**M4. Meshgrid/subgrid construction for the model backplanes.** Section 2.1 says build the incidence backplane "over the envelope bounding box" and occlusion backplanes "on the mask-bbox subgrid," calling it "the same call family `NavModelBody` uses." The mechanics — constructing an oops `Meshgrid`/`Backplane` restricted to a bbox subgrid and embedding the result into the full extended frame — are discoverable from `nav_model_body.py:585` and the surrounding `_render`, but the plan gives only the method name, not the subgrid-construction pattern. Moderate; a pointer to the `restr_meshgrid` construction would suffice.

## 5. Overall assessment

**Could I execute this cold, phase by phase, with no other briefing?** Phase A: yes — it is pure array math, fully specified, with a strong test list; the subtle recenter/double-count logic is completely pinned down and the acceptance is self-contained. Phase C and Phase F: yes, low risk. Phase E: yes in mechanics (the cohort source at `/seti/all_repos/rms-csmithing/tests/titan_images.txt` exists and is exactly the stated 87 lines), though its outcomes are inherently uncertain — that is expected for a validation phase, not a plan defect.

**The phase I'd worry about most is Phase B.** Two of its instructions are under-specified in ways that will stop an implementer rather than merely slow one: (1) it directs model decline-gate testing through `FakeObs`, which cannot supply the oops geometry those gates consume, with no stated injection strategy (M1); and (2) it prescribes an occluder-helper signature that cannot reproduce the existing `NavModelBody` occluder bit-identically (A1/I1), while asserting that it will. Both are the kind of thing that reads fine until you sit down to write the code. Phase D is a close second because the symmetry-breaking sim keys — the whole point of the adversarial sweep — are named but not specified as rendering math (M3), and the plan's acceptance bounds silently depend on them working.

Everything else is executable as written. The plan is unusually rigorous on the geometry and the failure-mode taxonomy; its weak points are concentrated at the library-integration seams (obs geometry, the occluder refactor, sim rendering) rather than in the algorithm itself.
